Understanding Breast Fibroadenoma
Removal
Fibroadenomas are non-cancerous, solid breast lumps made of
glandular and connective tissue. They are common, especially in
younger women, and while generally harmless, evaluation is important
to confirm diagnosis and guide management.
Many people considering Breast Fibroadenoma Removal have
questions or feel a little unsure about where to start — that's completely
normal. Our team takes time during your first visit to explain things in
plain language, so you can make a confident, informed decision at your
own pace.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is fibroadenoma cancer? Most fibroadenomas are benign, but proper
evaluation is important to confirm diagnosis.
Does the fibroadenoma always need to be removed? Not always —
your surgeon will advise based on size, symptoms, and growth pattern.
Is the procedure painful? Most patients feel minimal discomfort during
Breast Fibroadenoma Removal itself, since it is performed under
anesthesia. Some soreness in the days after is normal and manageable
with prescribed medication.
How long does the surgery take? This depends on the extent of the
procedure and will be explained clearly during your consultation, once
your specific plan is finalized.
When will I see the final results? You'll notice initial changes as
swelling reduces over the first few weeks, with final results becoming
clearer over the following months.
Do all fibroadenomas need to be removed? Not necessarily — small,
stable, and clearly benign fibroadenomas are sometimes simply
monitored; your surgeon will guide you based on imaging and clinical
findings.
Will removal affect breastfeeding later? Careful surgical planning
aims to minimize any impact on future breastfeeding ability; this is
discussed during consultation.
