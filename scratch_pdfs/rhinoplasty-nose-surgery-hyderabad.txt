Understanding Rhinoplasty
Rhinoplasty, or nose reshaping surgery, modifies the size, shape, or proportions of the nose
to improve facial balance. It can be purely cosmetic or combined with functional correction
to improve breathing.
Many people considering Rhinoplasty have questions or feel a little unsure about where to
start — that's completely normal. Our team takes time during your first visit to explain things
in plain language, so you can make a confident, informed decision at your own pace.

Additional Details You Should Know

Bone Grafts & Implants in Rhinoplasty
For patients needing significant structural change — such as building up a low bridge or
providing support after revision surgery — your surgeon may use a bone or cartilage graft
(often taken from the patient's own septum, ear, or rib) or, in select cases, a surgical implant.
These techniques add structure and support while maintaining a natural-looking result, and
the right choice depends on your nasal anatomy and goals.

Scars & Marks
Most rhinoplasty is performed with incisions hidden inside the nose; when external incisions
are needed (open technique), they are placed in the small strip of skin between the nostrils
and heal to be very discreet.
Safety & Things to Consider

Frequently Asked Questions

Does rhinoplasty affect breathing?
Functional rhinoplasty can address both appearance and breathing concerns.
Is the procedure painful?
Most patients feel minimal discomfort during Rhinoplasty itself, since it is performed under
anesthesia. Some soreness in the days after is normal and manageable with prescribed
medication.
How long does the surgery take?
This depends on the extent of the procedure and will be explained clearly during your
consultation, once your specific plan is finalized.
When will I see the final results?
You'll notice initial changes as swelling reduces over the first few weeks, with final results
becoming clearer over the following months.
How long until swelling fully goes down?
Most visible swelling resolves within a few weeks, but subtle refinement of the nasal tip can
continue for up to a year.
Can rhinoplasty fix a deviated septum?
Yes, functional rhinoplasty can correct a deviated septum at the same time as reshaping the
nose, improving both breathing and appearance.
