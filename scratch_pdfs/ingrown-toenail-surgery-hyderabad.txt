Understanding Ingrown Toenail Surgery
An ingrown toenail occurs when the edge of the nail grows into the surrounding skin,
causing pain, swelling, and sometimes infection. Surgery removes the offending nail edge or
portion permanently.
Many people considering Ingrown Toenail Surgery have questions or feel a little unsure
about where to start — that's completely normal. Our team takes time during your first visit
to explain things in plain language, so you can make a confident, informed decision at your
own pace.

Additional Details You Should Know
Preventing Recurrence

Along with removing the problematic nail edge, your surgeon may treat the nail bed
(matrixectomy) to reduce the likelihood of the same section of nail becoming ingrown again
in the future.

Frequently Asked Questions
Will I need general anesthesia?
Most minor procedures like this are done comfortably under local anesthesia, so you stay
awake but feel no pain in the area.
Will there be a visible scar?
Incisions are small and carefully placed to minimize visible scarring, and they typically fade
well over time.
Will I be able to walk immediately after?
Most patients can walk with mild care right after the procedure, though comfortable
footwear is recommended for a few days.
