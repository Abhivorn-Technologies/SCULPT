Understanding Lipoma Removal
A lipoma is a soft, benign, slow-growing lump of fat cells that forms beneath the skin. While
generally harmless, removal may be recommended for cosmetic reasons, growth, or
discomfort.
Many people considering Lipoma Removal have questions or feel a little unsure about where
to start — that's completely normal. Our team takes time during your first visit to explain
things in plain language, so you can make a confident, informed decision at your own pace.

Additional Details You Should Know
Lipomas vs. Cysts
While a lipoma is a soft lump of fat cells beneath the skin, a cyst is typically a fluid- or keratin-
filled sac. Both are usually benign but are treated slightly differently — your surgeon will

confirm the type through examination (and, if needed, imaging) before recommending the
right removal technique.

Frequently Asked Questions
Will I need general anesthesia?
Most minor procedures like this are done comfortably under local anesthesia, so you stay
awake but feel no pain in the area.
Will there be a visible scar?
Incisions are small and carefully placed to minimize visible scarring, and they typically fade
well over time.

Can a lipoma or cyst come back after removal?
Complete removal significantly reduces the chance of recurrence at the same site, though
new lipomas can occasionally develop elsewhere.
Is a biopsy needed after removal?
Removed tissue is often sent for routine evaluation to confirm the diagnosis, especially for
larger or unusual-looking lumps.
