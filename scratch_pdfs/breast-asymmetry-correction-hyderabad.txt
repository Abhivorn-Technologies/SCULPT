Understanding Breast Asymmetry

Correction
Breast asymmetry refers to a noticeable difference between the two
breasts in size, shape, volume, or position. While mild asymmetry is
common and normal, significant differences can be addressed
surgically.
Many people considering Breast Asymmetry Correction have questions
or feel a little unsure about where to start — that's completely normal.
Our team takes time during your first visit to explain things in plain
language, so you can make a confident, informed decision at your own
pace.
Signs You May Be a Good Candidate
You might be a good fit for this treatment if any of the following sound
familiar:
•  Women with a noticeable size or shape difference between
breasts
•  Those with asymmetry following pregnancy, weight change, or
development
•  Patients seeking a more balanced, proportionate appearance
The best way to know for sure is a one-on-one consultation, where your
surgeon reviews your health history and goals in detail.

Scar placement and extent depend on the technique used — implant or

lift approach involves more surgical incision, planned to be as
inconspicuous as possible.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is breast asymmetry normal? Mild asymmetry is common, but
significant differences can be corrected with customized procedures.
Is the procedure painful? Most patients feel minimal discomfort during
Breast Asymmetry Correction itself, since it is performed under
anesthesia. Some soreness in the days after is normal and manageable
with prescribed medication.
How long does the surgery take? This depends on the extent of the
procedure and will be explained clearly during your consultation, once
your specific plan is finalized.
When will I see the final results? You'll notice initial changes as
swelling reduces over the first few weeks, with final results becoming
clearer over the following months.
Will my insurance cover asymmetry correction? This depends on the
underlying cause and your individual insurance policy; our team can help
you understand what documentation may be needed.
At what age can asymmetry correction be considered? It's generally
recommended to wait until breast development is complete, which your
surgeon will assess during consultation.
